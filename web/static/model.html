How the model works

Every number on this page comes from molecules the model never saw while it was learning. Here you can check how good it really is, and where it fails.

Two kinds of exam

Random split: shuffle all molecules and hide 20% for the test. Test molecules usually have a close "cousin" in the training set, so this is the easier exam.

Scaffold split: group molecules by their ring skeleton and hide whole groups. The test molecules have skeletons the model has never seen, which is closer to predicting a brand-new drug.

Human vs machine

In 2004 John Delaney wrote a 4-term formula by hand: logS = 0.16 − 0.63·logP − 0.0062·MW + 0.066·RB − 0.74·AP. It is surprisingly good. The machine-learning models use more information and make … smaller errors on AqSolDB.

What the model looks at

Average size of each feature's push (mean |SHAP|) over all training molecules. "Fragment" features are Morgan fingerprint bits; the SMILES shows the small piece of molecule each bit stands for.
